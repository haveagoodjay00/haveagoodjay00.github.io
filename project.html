    Pre-registration summary

    Fixed before any calculation. Results are compared against this page, and the criteria are not changed after the results are seen. Changes are added to a change log with the reason, never edited in place.

    • Question. How do module size (20/50/100 MW) and connection structure change N-1 security, utilization balance, losses and the number of modules that fit? Case A (new town) against Case B (existing Seoul/Gyeonggi area).
    • Compared. Radial or ring inside the campus, single or dual supply. Radial with single supply is the baseline. T-tap variants (trunk load 0/30/60%, tap at 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 of the line) are tested too.
    • Cases. Up to 390 for Case B and 219 for Case A.
    • Verdict. Tier 1: each transformer out over 8,760 hours, judged at 100/120/150/200% of rating. Tier 2: transformer or one 154 kV line out at peak, with overload and ±10% voltage checks. N-1 is a gate; designs that pass are reported side by side, with no weights.
    • Three separate questions. AC convergence, physical limits met, and safety buffer met are reported separately.
    • Drop rule. If T-tap changes neither the N-1 ranking nor the number of modules that fit, it moves to an appendix as a null result.
    • Check rule. Hand calculation, NumPy DC and pandapower agree within 5% on the small test network, or I explain the gap.
    • My predictions. Radial breaks first. Moving the tap point does not change the ranking. Ring with single supply does not overtake radial with dual supply as modules grow. T-tap has a clear effect (my stated chance of "negligible": 0%). Each is scored right, partly right or wrong after the runs.

    What this study is not

    • A comparison within the structures and sizes I test, not a global optimum.
    • A model calibrated with Korean public data, not an analysis of the actual Korean grid.
    • Without cost data, "efficient" means efficient in power terms only.
